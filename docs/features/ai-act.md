# AI and the AI Act

Added in Thelia 3.3.

:::caution This guide is not legal advice
It explains in plain words what the European AI Act asks of a shop that uses artificial intelligence, and what Thelia does to help. It reflects the texts as read in October 2026. For your own situation, ask a lawyer.
:::

## What Thelia's AI layer is

Thelia 3.3 lets modules use a language model: a chat that answers visitors, a tool that writes a page, a job that compares prices. You connect your own account with a provider, give each kind of use a budget, and Thelia keeps a log of every call and what it cost.

Each thing a module does with a model is called a usage. A usage has a name such as `commerceagents.shopping_assistant`, an audience (visitors, customers, administrators or no one in particular) and a purpose in one sentence. You switch usages on and off one by one.

## What the AI Act says about transparency

The AI Act is the European regulation (EU) 2024/1689. Its article 50 is about transparency and has applied since 2 August 2026. For a shop it comes down to four situations.

| Situation | What is asked |
| --- | --- |
| A visitor talks with a chatbot (article 50(1)) | Tell the person, from the first exchange, that they are talking with an AI. A line in the terms and conditions is not enough. |
| A system generates content (article 50(2)) | The content is marked as generated, in a form a machine can read (metadata, for example). Plain assistance with editing, or a change that does not alter the content substantially, is left out. |
| You publish AI-generated text to inform the public on matters of public interest (article 50(4)) | Either a label saying it was generated by an AI, or a human review with someone editorially responsible for the text. |
| Product descriptions and advertising | Outside this duty. The exception is a claim about health, safety or sustainability: then the previous line applies. |

Dates:

- Article 50 has applied since 2 August 2026.
- For a system that was already on the market before that date, the marking of generated content (50(2)) is due on 2 December 2026.

The regulation sets a fine of up to 15 million euros or 3 % of turnover for a breach of article 50 (article 99(4)). In France the authority in charge has not been settled: the law is not yet promulgated.

Two other points are worth knowing:

- Open source does not exempt a system from article 50. The Commission guidelines on article 50 say it explicitly.
- Whoever puts a system into service under their own name or brand is its provider, free of charge or not. A shop that publishes a chat under its own name is in that position.

## What Thelia gives you

Thelia supplies tools and explains. It does not stop an AI usage because of the AI Act, and it does not decide for you.

| Tool | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| Notice "you are talking with an AI" | On by default for every usage that declares a conversation with a person. The module shows it before the person writes. You can turn it off per usage. |
| Provenance mark | On by default for every usage that generates published content. Each generated content carries a record: that it is AI-generated, which usage, which model, when, and the number of the log row. The module stores it with the content and exposes it as metadata. You can turn it off per usage. |
| Compliance status per usage | Each usage shows whether its duty is met, not met (you turned the tool off) or to be reviewed (a usage open to visitors or customers whose module did not say whether people talk with it). The status informs; the usage keeps running. |
| Register of AI usages | A list of every usage in the shop: module, purpose, audience, provider, model, obligations and status. It can be exported, and it is the record of how AI is used in your shop. |
| Log | Every call is logged with its usage, provider, model, tokens, cost and a hashed identifier of who made it. The text of the prompts is not stored unless you turn that on for a usage, with a retention period. |

## Providers and where data goes

Each connection to a provider shows where the provider processes the data: in the European Union or outside it.

| Provider | Jurisdiction shown by default |
| --- | --- |
| Mistral | European Union |
| Scaleway | European Union |
| OVHcloud | European Union |
| Albert (French State) | European Union |
| Thelia AI preset | European Union |
| OpenAI | Outside the EU, unless the connection uses the European endpoint `https://eu.api.openai.com` |
| Anthropic | Outside the EU |
| Ollama, compatible endpoint | You declare it: it is where you host the server |

Thelia prefers European providers: they are the defaults it proposes. It does not forbid the others. A connection outside the EU works and shows a warning about the transfer of data outside the EU.

If you want to rule them out, an option "EU only" refuses any connection and any outside service that processes data outside the EU. It is off by default. With it on, a module that depends on such a service stops working until you turn the option off.

## Your part

The tools do not make a shop compliant by themselves. What stays with you:

- Leave the notice and the mark on, or accept that the status shows the usage as not compliant.
- Check that the module you use displays the notice on every state of its interface. A module has to do it; the notice comes from Thelia.
- For text you publish to inform the public (news, guides, public-interest pages), add a label or have a person review it and answer for it. The mark in the metadata does not replace this.
- Check any claim on health, safety or sustainability in a description or an advertisement, because those fall back under the duty.
- Review what a chat tells visitors: no promise it cannot keep, no false urgency.
- Set budgets and the monthly cap, and read the log. A usage open to anonymous visitors stays inactive until you give it a budget above zero.
- Keep the register of AI usages up to date for your own records. It documents what you deploy.
- Say in your privacy policy that visitors' messages go to the provider you chose, and choose that provider knowing where it processes data.

One case the AI Act treats more strictly is not supported by Thelia: usages that rank people for access to credit, for instance an automatic assessment of the solvency of a customer for payment in several instalments, fall in the high-risk category. Do not build such a usage on the AI layer.
