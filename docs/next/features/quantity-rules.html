Quantity rules and units of measure

Added in Thelia 3.4.

Each combination can set rules on the quantity a customer may buy, and a unit of measure. This covers a product sold by the lot ("by 6"), a product with a minimum order, and a product sold by weight, volume or length ("1.25 kg").

A shop changes nothing until a combination is given rules. The defaults are the behaviour every combination already had: a minimum of 1, a step of 1, sold by the piece.

Rules of a combination​

In the Combinations tab of a product, each combination has these fields:

FieldColumnDefaultMeaning
Minimum quantitymin_quantity1The smallest quantity sold.
Sold byquantity_step1Every quantity is a multiple of it.
Decimals of a quantityquantity_decimals0From 0 to 3. 0 is sold by the piece. More than 0 is sold by measure.
Unitunit_codenoneOne of pc, kg, g, l, ml, m, cm, m2, m3.
Content of one unit soldunit_quantitynoneWhat one unit sold contains, in the unit: 0.75 for a 75 cl bottle sold in litres.
Price shown perreference_unit_quantitynoneThe quantity the reference price is given for: 1 for a price per litre.

The form shows the resulting Price per reference unit, incl. tax. A bottle of 75 cl at 9.99 gives 13.32 per litre. The button Apply these settings to every combination and save copies the rules to all the combinations of the product.

Changing a rule adjusts the quantities of the carts in progress, the next time they are read.

Examples:

  • A pack sold by 6, at least 6: minimum 6, step 6, 0 decimals. Valid quantities are 6, 12, 18.
  • Cheese sold by weight, to the gram, from 100 g: unit kg, 3 decimals, minimum 0.1, step 0.001.
  • A bottle of 0.75 l sold by the piece with a price per litre: 0 decimals, unit l, content 0.75, reference 1.

A rule that cannot decide a quantity is refused: a step or a minimum that is not positive, more than 3 decimals, a step or a minimum with more decimals than the combination allows, a unit quantity that is not positive, or an unknown unit.

What the customer sees​

The rules hold everywhere a quantity is written.

  • Adding to the cart: a quantity off the rules is refused with a message that gives the valid quantities ("Sold by 6: 6, 12, 18..."). A quantity that is zero or less is refused too. The check is made on what the line will hold once the addition joins it.
  • Reading a cart back (restored from its cookie, merged at sign-in, rules changed since): the cart does not refuse. It brings a line down to the valid quantity below it, or removes it when even that is under the minimum. The checkout then stops once, so the customer sees the new quantities before paying.
  • Offered lines (a promotion, a free product coupon) are held to no rule.
  • The quick order and purchase lists refuse a line off the rules and a combination sold by measure, which has to be ordered from its product page.

On an order, the unit and the decimals are frozen on each line when the order is placed. Totals are charged in cents, as the cart rounds them: 1.25 kg at 14.82 is charged 18.53.

Stock moves on the thousandth, so ten orders of 10 g taken from 10 tonnes leave exactly 9999.9 kg. A stock written by the back office, an import, the generation of combinations or the API holds the decimals of its combination. 12.5 pieces of a combination sold by the piece is refused. The minimum and the step apply to a cart line, not to a stock.

The Flexy theme sells by these rules from the Flexy release that follows Thelia 3.4: the product sheet starts at the minimum, offers a lot as a list, steps a quantity by its step, and shows the price per reference unit ("13,32 EUR / l") on the sheet and the product card. A custom theme does this with the classes below.

Developer reference​

API​

/api/admin/product_sale_elements, and the combinations of /api/admin/products, write minQuantity, quantityStep, quantityDecimals, unitQuantity, referenceUnitQuantity and unitCode. A refused rule answers 422. The front API reads them.

POST and PUT /api/front/cart_items judge the quantity by the rules. A quantity of 7 on a combination sold by 6 answers 422 with the valid quantities. 1.25 on a combination sold to the hundredth is taken. 1.5 on a combination sold by the piece answers 422.

quantity is a float on ProductSaleElements, since a stock can carry decimals. On the order lines of the API, unitCode and quantityDecimals are read only. /api/admin/cart_items does not write the quantity of a line.

Classes​

All in Thelia\Domain\Catalog\Quantity.

ClassRole
UnitCodeThe units, with their symbol and translated name.
QuantityRule::fromProductSaleElements()Reads the rules of a loaded combination, without a query. Throws InvalidQuantityRuleException for a rule that cannot decide a quantity.
QuantityPolicy::assertAcceptable()Returns the quantity, or throws QuantityRefusedException with a translated message that lists the valid ones. For paths where the buyer can change the quantity.
QuantityPolicy::normalize()Returns a QuantityDecision: the valid multiple below the quantity, or the removal of the line (removesTheLine()), with the reason and a translated message().
QuantityPolicy::parse()Reads "1,25" and "1.25". Refuses an input that could be read two ways, such as "1.250" on a combination sold with fewer than three decimals (UnreadableQuantityException).
QuantityPolicy::assertStockFits()Judges a stock against the decimals of the combination.
ReferenceUnitPriceCalculator::compute()The price per reference unit.
QuantityFormatter::format(), formatMeasure()Write a quantity with its decimals in a language. format() adds the unit only for a combination sold by measure. formatMeasure() always adds it.

assertAcceptable(), normalize() and parse() take a combination or a QuantityRule. Both assertAcceptable() and normalize() throw NotPositiveQuantityException for zero or less.

Cart​

CartItemAddDTO and CartItemUpdateQuantityDTO take a float quantity, and CartEvent keeps it as it is, where it used to cut it to a whole number. Action\Cart::addItem() and changeItem() throw Thelia\Domain\Cart\Exception\CartQuantityRefusedException, an InvalidCartException, so every caller (the CartAdd form, CartFacade, the front API, a theme) gets the same refusal.

Thelia\Domain\Cart\Service\CartQuantityReconciler adjusts a cart read back, and dispatches TheliaEvents::CART_ITEM_QUANTITY_ADJUSTED (CartItemQuantityAdjustedEvent, with the QuantityDecision and a message). The cart step of the checkout then throws CartQuantitiesAdjustedException, code cart-quantities-adjusted.

Templates and loops​

The core declares the format_quantity Twig filter, also for the e-mail and PDF templates:

{{ line.QUANTITY|format_quantity(line.QUANTITY_DECIMALS, line.UNIT_CODE) }}
{# "1,25 kg" in French #}

The arguments are quantity|format_quantity(decimals, unitCode, locale). The locale is the one of the current request when none is given. A theme that declares its own filter keeps it.

The order_product loop outputs UNIT_CODE and QUANTITY_DECIMALS (empty and 0 on a line placed before). The return document context gains the same two keys on each entry of lines.

Storage​

Quantities and stocks are DOUBLE columns, where they were single precision FLOAT. A stock of 1234.567 kg is read back as 1234.567. The 3.4.0.sql update script converts the columns. A module that reads these columns in SQL gets the double precision value. The getters and setters stay float.

Upgrading​

  • CartEvent::getQuantity() returns ?float instead of ?int. A listener that compares it to an integer with ===, or passes it to an int parameter, has to follow.
  • Order::getTotalAmount() and the cart line totals round to the cent, half up. An order with whole quantities totals the same amounts.
  • A Flexy child theme that reads a quantity or a stock into an int has to read a float.
