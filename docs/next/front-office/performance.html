Performance

In short. The core measures the Flexy storefront with Lighthouse on every change of its main branch, every night and every release, and fails when a page gets worse than its written reference. The figures below are those of the reference. They are lab figures of a demo shop: they compare one version of Thelia with another, not your shop with another.

On mobile, the performance score, FCP and LCP of one measure can land far from these medians: on the same code they jump between two values. See what is not held by a threshold.

The home page of the demo is a page of the CMS, with a short text and three teasers.

The product page is the weak point: its first gallery image, the largest element of the page, is loaded lazily and has no dimensions, and the information block below it moves when the images arrive. The search results page scores 66 in SEO because it asks not to be indexed, on purpose.

Scores are out of 100. FCP and LCP are the times until the first content and the largest content are painted, CLS the layout shift, TBT the time the main thread is blocked during the load. Transferred is the weight sent over the network, compressed; Requests the number of requests the page makes.

INP, the interaction metric of the Core Web Vitals, needs a real visitor clicking: Lighthouse measures TBT instead, which moves with the JavaScript run at load.

How the figures are measured​

ShopFresh install with the demo data (bin/install --with-demo): about thirty products
ModeAPP_ENV=prod, assets compiled and precompressed by asset-map:compile
ServerApache and PHP-FPM, PHP 8.3, over HTTP/2 with TLS, with the public/.htaccess of the release
MachineGitHub-hosted runner, 4 vCPU Intel(R) Xeon(R) 6975P-C, 15 GB
ToolLighthouse 12.6.1 through Lighthouse CI 0.15.1, in the Chrome of Puppeteer 25.16.0
PresetsDesktop (preset: desktop) and mobile (Lighthouse default: simulated slow 4G and a slower CPU)
Runs3 per page and preset; a measure keeps the median of the three. The reference is the median of four measures
PagesThe ten pages of setup/audit/lighthouserc.default.yaml. The four account pages are measured signed in as the demo customer, through the sign-in form

Before measuring, the job checks that Flexy is the active theme, that the debug toolbar is off, and that a stylesheet is served from its precompressed copy. A page that redirects fails the measure: an account page measured signed out would be the sign-in form under another name.

The workflow is .github/workflows/lighthouse.yml of the core. Each release carries the figures measured on it, as lighthouse.md and two JSON files attached to the release on GitHub.

Measure it yourself​

On a shop of yours, or on a fresh install with the demo data, served in production:

npm install --prefix /tmp/lhci @lhci/cli@0.15.1 puppeteer@25.16.0
export PATH="/tmp/lhci/node_modules/.bin:$PATH"

mkdir -p /tmp/lighthouse/desktop
node setup/audit/lighthouse.mjs config --origin https://my-shop.test --preset desktop \
  --output /tmp/lighthouse/desktop/lighthouserc.yaml

export LIGHTHOUSE_CUSTOMER_EMAIL=test@thelia.net LIGHTHOUSE_CUSTOMER_PASSWORD=thelia
cd /tmp/lighthouse/desktop && lhci collect --config=lighthouserc.yaml && cd -

node setup/audit/lighthouse.mjs summary --preset desktop --results /tmp/lighthouse/desktop/.lighthouseci

Run Lighthouse CI from the folder of the generated file: it writes its results in .lighthouseci there, and loads the sign-in script relative to it. Use a customer of your own shop for the account pages. --preset mobile gives the mobile figures.

On another machine the figures move: a faster processor lowers the times, a slower network raises them. Compare a measure with another made on the same machine, and read the scores of this page as an order of magnitude. On mobile, compare the times and the performance score with the ranges of what is not held by a threshold, not with a single median: one measure lands on either of two values.

The thresholds​

setup/audit/lighthouse-budget.json holds, for each page and preset, the reference measure with its date and commit, and a tolerance per metric. The job fails when a median is worse than the reference moved by the tolerance, and names the page and the metric:

The tolerances are set from the gaps between measures of the same code on GitHub runners, so that the noise of a shared machine does not fail the job. On desktop the times moved by 25 % at most over seven measures (0.68 to 0.85 s on the content page), inside their tolerance, and the desktop performance score by 4 points at most. The accessibility, best practices and SEO scores, the layout shift, the blocking time, the weight and the request count did not move, except the mobile layout shift of the product page, and keep tight thresholds on both presets.

What is not held by a threshold​

Some figures move too much between two measures of the same code to tell a regression from the noise. They are measured and published like the others, but fail nothing; the summary of each measure lists them under its table, with the reason.

  • Mobile FCP, LCP and performance score. Lighthouse simulates the slow 4G of the mobile preset from what it recorded during the load. The Google Fonts files of Flexy come from the internet, and finish before or after the real first paint from one run to the next; when they finish before, the simulation counts them, and the times jump by about 1.5 s. Over the 21 runs per page of 2026-11-17, the mobile FCP is 1.68 s when none had finished (median of 52 runs) and 3.18 s when all seven had (median of 91 runs). The performance score follows the times. More runs do not help: a median of 15 runs per page still moves by up to 62 %.
  • Desktop LCP and mobile CLS of the product page. Its largest element is the first gallery image, loaded lazily: it is painted early or late, 0.83 to 1.49 s between two measures on desktop. On mobile, the gallery images, which have no dimensions, shift the page or not before Lighthouse stops recording: the CLS is 0.928 or 0 from one run to the next.

The reference is changed by hand, in a commit that says why: after an improvement is acquired, to keep it, or when a change accepts a cost and says so. It is never rewritten from the last run, which would let a slow drift through. summary prints the values of a run in the format of the budget file, ready to be reviewed and pasted.

Limits​

  • Lab, not field. These are measures in a controlled browser, not the experience of real visitors on their devices. The field figures of a shop come from its own visitors (Chrome User Experience Report, a RUM tool).
  • A demo shop. Thirty products, a few categories, no third-party script, no tracking, no chat. A shop with a large catalogue or many modules has other figures; how the theme holds with volume is a separate measure.
  • A shared machine. GitHub runners do not have constant performance. A measure made there compares with another made there.
  • The accessibility score is an indicator. It counts automated checks; the accessibility statement is what tells the conformance of the theme.
